§ 01 · Abstract

One representation, three roles

Embodied AI aims to develop agents capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting in physical environments. Beyond recognizing objects and scenes, these agents must identify possible interactions, determine which are relevant to the current task and embodiment, and translate them into executable behavior. Affordance learning supports this process by grounding perception in action possibilities and connecting it with task reasoning and control. Existing surveys, however, have largely adopted either a perception-centered or robotics-centered perspective, leaving how affordance perception, reasoning, and action jointly contribute to embodied intelligence underexplored.

To bridge this gap, this survey presents a unified framework that organizes the literature around three complementary roles: affordance perception identifies what and where interaction is possible; affordance reasoning selects opportunities relevant to the task, context, and agent state; and affordance-guided action uses affordance representations to support planning and policy learning. Within this framework, we develop fine-grained taxonomies, systematically compare representative methods, and trace the field's progression from passive visual recognition toward action-aware embodied learning. We further organize representative datasets and evaluation protocols, and outline open challenges and future directions.

§ 03 · Evolution

Five paradigm shifts

Affordance learning has moved from supervised visual prediction of action regions to action-grounded embodied learning. Five shifts carry that arc.

  1. 01dense labelsdiverse evidence

    Image-level labels, sparse points, human–object interactions, model priors, and physical simulation complement dense masks; reliability depends on evidence quality and simulation fidelity.

  2. 022D image plane3D metric space

    From view-dependent heatmaps to metric grounding on point clouds and Gaussian splats.

  3. 03static regionsactionable dynamics

    Beyond masks: contact points, motion directions, poses, and post-contact trajectories.

  4. 04closed-set recognitionopen-set generalization

    Fixed vocabularies dissolve into language queries and foundation-model grounding.

  5. 05modular pipelinesintegrated policies

    Affordance moves inside diffusion and VLA policies — as condition, representation, or reward.

§ 06 · Open problems

Challenges & future directions

Five research directions toward scalable, reliable, and actionable affordance learning for open-world embodied interaction.

  1. 7.1

    Affordance representation and action integration

    Explicit masks, contacts, and trajectories offer interpretable action interfaces; latent features, auxiliary objectives, and optimization signals support other forms of policy integration. Controlled comparisons should match observations, training data, policy backbones, and action interfaces, test no-affordance, predicted-affordance, and oracle-affordance conditions, and use interventions to establish whether affordance information improves execution.

  2. 7.2

    Scalable learning and open-world generalization

    Human interactions, demonstrations, model-generated labels, simulation, and robot experience broaden supervision, but each introduces uncertainty and bias. Future data pipelines should control quality while varying objects, tasks, states, and environments. Affordance-guided data generation and predictive models can scale interaction examples while preserving transferable functional relations.

  3. 7.3

    Reliable and autonomous affordance grounding

    Multi-stage grounding can propagate early errors and depend heavily on prompts, thresholds, tool choices, and fallback rules. Future systems should select evidence and tools adaptively, track provenance and uncertainty, verify semantic and spatial consistency, and retry when needed. Evaluation should report human corrections, intermediate failures, tool calls, latency, cost, and sensitivity to component replacement.

  4. 7.4

    Closed-loop affordance modeling and planning

    Affordances change as actions alter object states, visibility, and scene configuration. Agents should maintain and update affordance beliefs through observation, action, and execution feedback. Active sensing and low-risk exploration can resolve uncertainty; long-horizon planning should model affordance transitions, track progress, revise later choices, and recover from unexpected outcomes.

  5. 7.5

    Embodied evaluation, safety, and trustworthiness

    Perceptual agreement does not establish whether an interaction is reachable, stable, or compatible with a particular robot. Benchmarks should measure embodiment-specific feasibility, execution success, and uncertainty calibration, using validated simulation alongside robot trials. Evaluation should also test complementary sensory evidence, unsafe and negative affordances, and whether actions respect human intent and social norms.
